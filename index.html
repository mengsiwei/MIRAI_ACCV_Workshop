Aim & Scope

MIRAI Driving brings together researchers from computer vision, machine learning, robotics, intelligent transportation systems, and industry to discuss recent advances in multimodal intelligence and reasoning for autonomous driving. Modern intelligent vehicles increasingly rely on diverse information sources such as cameras, LiDAR, radar, HD maps, vehicle telemetry, user input, and V2X communication. These multimodal signals provide rich information for perception, prediction, planning, control, and decision making.

Autonomous driving nevertheless remains challenging due to long-tail scenarios, rare safety-critical events, domain shifts, complex human–vehicle interactions, and the need for robust reasoning in previously unseen environments. Recent advances in foundation models, large language models, vision–language–action models, and world models offer new opportunities to address these challenges, reshaping the development of next-generation autonomous driving systems through stronger multimodal perception, reasoning, planning, and interaction capabilities.

This workshop provides a focused forum for discussing multimodal intelligence, foundation models for driving, world models, V2X intelligence, digital twins, trustworthy AI, human–vehicle interaction, and end-to-end driving systems. It is organized as a half-day invited event featuring keynote talks, invited presentations, and an interactive panel discussion — a format intended to encourage in-depth, high-quality exchange between academia and industry rather than a competition or proceedings track.

This is an invited workshop. There is no call for papers and no proceedings track. Attendance is open to all registered ACCV 2026 participants — no separate registration is required beyond the main conference.

Topics of Interest

Discussion at MIRAI Driving centers on, but is not limited to, the following themes.

🌸

Multimodal Perception & Fusion

Camera, LiDAR, radar, HD map, telemetry, and language fusion for robust scene understanding.

🧠

Foundation Models for Driving

LLMs, VLMs, and vision–language–action models applied to perception, planning, and control.

🌍

World Models & Simulation

Generative world models, driving scene synthesis, and closed-loop evaluation.

📡

V2X & Cooperative Intelligence

Vehicle-to-everything communication, cooperative perception, and multi-agent intelligence.

🏙️

Digital Twins for Mobility

High-fidelity digital twins for testing, validation, and intelligent transportation systems.

🛡️

Trustworthy & Safe Autonomy

Robustness, uncertainty, interpretability, and safety under long-tail and low-quality data.

🤝

Human–Vehicle Interaction

Human-autonomy teaming, driver modeling, and interaction-aware decision making.

🚗

End-to-End Driving Systems

Unified differentiable pipelines from sensing to action, and embodied driving agents.

Invited Keynote Speakers

Three keynote speakers have been invited to present at MIRAI Driving.

Prof. Joshua Li

Oklahoma State University, USA

Williams Professor · School of Civil and Environmental Engineering

Prof. Joshua Q. Li is Williams Professor in the School of Civil and Environmental Engineering at Oklahoma State University. He received his Ph.D. from the University of Arkansas. His research covers automated infrastructure condition evaluation, sensor instrumentation and data automation, connected and autonomous transportation, transportation safety, big data analytics, transportation asset management, and driving behaviors and simulation. He has authored over 100 publications in leading journals and international conferences, and his research has been supported by the U.S. Department of Transportation and numerous state agencies. He serves as an Associate Editor for the ASCE Journal of Infrastructure Systems and the International Journal of Pavement Research and Technology.

Dr. Runsheng Xu

Waymo, USA

Senior Research Scientist

Dr. Runsheng Xu works on multimodal large language models for autonomous driving at Waymo. He received his Ph.D. from UCLA and previously worked at Mercedes-Benz R&D North America and OPPO R&D US. His research interests include autonomous driving, multimodal foundation models, diffusion models, and multi-agent intelligence. He has published more than 50 papers in leading venues including CVPR, ICCV, ECCV, ICRA, and IEEE Transactions, and has led influential open-source projects such as OpenCDA, OPV2V, V2V4Real, V2X-DiT, and WOD-E2E.

Prof. Takahiro Ogawa

Hokkaido University, Japan

Professor · Faculty of Information Science and Technology / Laboratory of Media Dynamics

Prof. Takahiro Ogawa is a Professor with the Faculty of Information Science and Technology, Hokkaido University, where he leads the Laboratory of Media Dynamics. He received his B.S., M.S., and Ph.D. degrees from Hokkaido University in 2003, 2005, and 2007, respectively. His research covers large-scale and diverse cross-media analysis and visualization, spanning images, video, music, medical and brain activity data, planetary imagery, infrastructure information, sports footage, and social media. His current work centers on multimodal AI, including vision–language models and vision–language–action models for Physical AI, as well as information retrieval and recommender systems, with applications to infrastructure inspection, medical image analysis, and industrial monitoring. He has authored more than 255 peer-reviewed journal articles and over 450 international conference papers. He served as Conference Chair of IEEE GCCE 2019 and as a Vice President of The Institute of Image Information and Television Engineers.
